CAPTION
Fresh snow is mostly air, 90 to 95 percent of it trapped between the ice crystals, and air that can't move carries very little heat. That's why a deep layer of snow works like an insulating blanket over whatever it covers, from a field to a barn roof. Barns and cabins outside Steamboat Springs, Colorado, and a covered bridge in Vermont.

Photographs: Carol M. Highsmith, Library of Congress.
