CAPTION
In 1962, Achille and Pier Giacomo Castiglioni wanted a light that could hang over a dining table without touching the ceiling. They borrowed the idea from a street lamp: a steel arc carries the shade out over the table, and a block of Carrara marble holds it from the corner. To move it, two people slide a broom handle through the hole in the marble and lift.

Arco (1962), Achille and Pier Giacomo Castiglioni for Flos. Photographs: The Museum of Modern Art, New York.
